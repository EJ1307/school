1Our approach

Why parents choose Hopscotch

We asked our parents what tipped the balance. The same four things came up again and again.

1:10

Small on purpose

No class is bigger than 20, and every early-years room has two teachers. That’s enough grown-ups to notice who’s quiet today — and to find out why.

Play is the work

Children count with beads and buttons, learn letters as sounds first, and work things out by building them. Our early years are inspired by Montessori and Reggio Emilia — practical, never precious.

Outside, every single day

At least 90 minutes a day in the sand pit, the splash pool, the garden and up the climbing frame. Monsoon included — there’s a rack of gumboots by the door.

Hello! नमस्कार! नमस्ते!

At home in three languages

English comes first, with Marathi and Hindi woven through songs, stories and the names of everything growing in our garden.

P.S. No homework until Grade 2. Evenings are for families.

How day care works

3A day here

A morning in the early years, more or less

Little ones do best when they know what’s coming next, so our mornings follow a gentle rhythm. Here’s a typical day in Nursery and KG.

8:45 Hello & free play

  1. 8:45

    Hello & free play

    Shoes off, name card on the board, then straight to the block corner. Or the sand pit. Usually the sand pit.

  2. 9:15

    Circle time

    Good-morning songs, today’s weather report and the big question: who brought something for show-and-tell?

  3. 9:45

    Discovery

    This month’s theme, hands-on: planting seeds, building bridges from blocks, sorting shells by size.

  4. 10:30

    Snack & story

    Fruit and something warm from our kitchen, then a story read slowly, with all the voices.

  5. 11:00

    Outdoors

    Sand, water and climbing. In the monsoon: gumboots, puddles and the covered play hall.

  6. 11:45

    Music & movement, or art

    Drums and dancing in the music room, or paint up to the elbows in the studio.

  7. 12:30

    Goodbye song

    Bags packed, one last song and a high-five at the gate. Day-care children stay on for lunch.

Primary children (Grades 1–5) stay until 2:30 pm, with lunch at 12:15.

4Campus

Built at child height

Sinks they can reach, books they can choose for themselves, and doors light enough to open alone. Everything here is made to be used, not admired.

  • that’s our splash pool!

    Splash pool

    Knee-deep, shaded, and only ever open with two adults on the side. Water-play days are the highlight of April and May.

  • Sand pit

    Big enough for six diggers at once. Sieved and topped up every term.

  • Library nook

    Eight hundred picture books, floor cushions and borrowing day every Friday.

  • Art studio

    Easels, clay and a sink at child height. Aprons strongly recommended.

  • Music & movement room

    A sprung floor, drums, a piano and a basket of very swishy scarves.

  • Kitchen garden

    Tomatoes, methi and marigolds — planted, watered and eventually eaten by the children.

  • Rainy-day play hall

    Covered, airy and big enough for tricycles when the monsoon sets in.

Safety and care, quietly built in

  • CCTV across campusClassrooms, corridors, play areas and both gates.
  • Police-verified staffEvery adult on campus, from teachers to drivers and cooks.
  • A nurse on campusFull-time during school hours, with a calm little sick bay.
  • GPS-tracked vansLive tracking on your phone, and a female attendant on every route.
  • Nut-free kitchenLunch and snacks cooked fresh here every morning.
